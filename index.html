Privacy Policy for Comb

Last updated: 22 September 2026

Comb is a puzzle game made by Graydentech (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what happens to information when you play it. It covers the Comb app for Android, package com.graydentech.comb.

Comb has no accounts, no sign-in and no servers of our own. The only reason any information leaves your device is to show advertising, and you choose how that works the first time you open the game.

1. The short version

  • Your progress and settings never leave your phone.
  • We do collect anonymous play statistics — which levels are started and finished, scores, and how many pieces were placed — through Google Analytics, so we can tell which levels are too hard. Nothing in it identifies you. Section 2 lists it in full.
  • Our advertising partner does collect some information, listed in full in section 3.
  • You choose, on first launch, whether ads may be personalised. You can change it at any time in Settings, and the game is identical either way.
  • We never sell your personal information.
  • Comb is not directed at children (see section 8).

2. What Comb itself stores and collects

On your device

Two files, both on your device only, both invisible to us:

WhatWhereContains
Game progress local app storage (comb_progress) levels completed, best scores, medals, and your accessibility and haptics settings
Your advertising choice local app storage (comb_consent) whether you allowed personalised ads, and the date you chose

These are never uploaded, never backed up to us, and are permanently deleted when you uninstall Comb or clear its data from Android Settings. We cannot read them and we cannot recover them.

We keep the date of your advertising choice because data-protection law requires us to be able to show that a choice was made, and when.

Play statistics, through Google Analytics

Comb uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count how the game is played. It is the only thing Comb sends anywhere of its own accord, and it exists for one purpose: telling which levels people give up on.

EventSent whenContains
level_start a level begins the level number, and whether it is a pack level or an encore level
level_end a level is won or lost the level number, pack or encore, whether it was won, and how many pieces were placed
post_score a level is won or lost the level number, pack or encore, and the score
ad_impression an advertisement is shown which ad network filled it, the format and slot, and what it earned

Google Analytics also records, automatically, the kind of information it records for every app that uses it: device model, operating system version, language, app version, and an app-instance identifier that is generated on your device and is reset if you clear Comb’s data or uninstall it. It does not receive your name, your email address, or anything you type — Comb has no accounts and no text to type.

Where you are. Comb itself never asks for your location and has no location permission. Google derives approximate location from the IP address your device connects from. We have switched city-level collection off, so the finest location recorded is your continent, country and region — never your city, and never coordinates. If you are in the EU, the UK or Switzerland, Google states that it “does not log or store individual IP addresses” and that the address is “used solely for geo-location data derivation before being immediately discarded”, with the lookup performed on servers in those regions (support.google.com/analytics/answer/12017362).

Nothing is collected before you answer the ad choice screen. Analytics is switched off in the app until you have answered it, so a person who opens Comb once and closes it on that screen produces no statistics at all. If you choose basic ads, statistics are still collected — being counted is not the same as being profiled — but Google is told it may not use any of it for advertising, ad measurement or ad personalisation.

Google’s handling of this data is described at firebase.google.com/support/privacy and policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

3. What our advertising partner receives

Comb shows ads through Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads, which are services of Unity Technologies. ironSource is part of Unity and is covered by the same policy.

When the game starts, and only after you have made your advertising choice, the following is sent to Unity so an ad can be selected and shown. This list was taken from the app’s own network activity on a real device:

  • Your advertising ID (Google Advertising ID), and whether you have limited ad tracking
  • Your device: manufacturer, model, operating system and version, free storage, battery level, and whether the device is rooted
  • Your country, language and time zone — not your precise location, and Comb requests no location permission of any kind
  • Your connection: mobile carrier and whether you are on Wi-Fi or mobile data
  • Technical identifiers for the app and the session, and the app version

If you choose personalised ads, Unity may use your advertising ID to build an interest profile and select ads based on it.

If you choose basic ads, your advertising ID is not used to profile you. The device and country details above are still sent, because they are needed to deliver any advertisement at all, and to prevent fraud and count views. This is why the app tells you that on the choice screen rather than claiming nothing is shared.

Unity’s handling of this information is governed by its own policy:
https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy

We have no access to any data Unity collects, and we cannot delete it on your behalf. Requests about that data go to Unity, using the contacts in their policy above.

4. Your choices

In the game. Open Settings → Privacy options to reopen the advertising choice screen at any time and change your answer. Choosing basic ads is free, immediate and costs you nothing: no feature is locked, no level is withheld, and no ad ever appears on the game board.

On your device. Android gives you controls that override anything an app does:

  • Settings → Privacy → Ads → Delete advertising ID removes the identifier entirely. Apps then cannot use it, and only non-personalised ads can be shown.
  • Settings → Privacy → Ads → Opt out of Ads Personalisation keeps the ID but signals that it must not be used for profiling.

The exact menu names vary between Android versions and phone makers.

5. Permissions Comb requests, and why

  • Internet and network state — to fetch advertisements. The game itself plays fully offline.
  • Advertising ID (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) — required by the advertising SDK.
  • The advertising SDK also adds permissions to run in the background, start after the device reboots and keep the device awake. These come from the Android WorkManager library bundled inside it, which it uses to finish its own tasks. Comb schedules no background work of its own.

Comb requests no access to location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, files or your list of installed apps.

6. Retention and deletion

  • On your device: progress, settings and your advertising choice remain until you uninstall Comb or clear its data. Both actions delete them immediately and permanently.
  • With us: nothing. We hold no database and no copy of anything.
  • With Google Analytics: event-level records are kept for 2 months and the app-instance identifier they are grouped under for 14 months, after which Google deletes them automatically. Aggregated reports — the counts we actually look at — are unaffected. Nothing here is tied to a name or an account; the identifier is created on your device and is destroyed when you uninstall Comb or clear its data.
  • With Unity: their retention periods are set out in their policy, linked in section 3.

Because there is no account, there is nothing for us to delete on request — uninstalling the app is complete deletion of everything we are involved with.

7. Security

Comb stores its two files in Android’s private per-app storage, which other apps cannot read. All advertising traffic uses encrypted connections provided by the advertising SDK. We do not transmit, receive or store any personal information ourselves, which is the strongest security measure available to us: there is nothing of yours for us to lose.

8. Children

Comb is rated for ages 16 and over and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children. The app is not enrolled in Google Play’s Designed for Families programme and does not use child-directed advertising treatment.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct, delete or object to the use of your personal information, and to withdraw consent at any time.

  • Withdrawing advertising consent is done in the game: Settings → Privacy options. It takes effect immediately and is as easy as giving it.
  • For the information Unity holds, use the request mechanisms in Unity’s privacy policy.
  • For anything else, contact us at the address below.

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising beyond the advertising described in section 3, which you control.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change how Comb handles information, we will update this policy and change the date at the top. If a change materially affects how your data is used for advertising, you will be asked for your choice again inside the game rather than only being told here.
